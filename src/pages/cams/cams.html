Cam and Follower

Introduction

Cams are used in a wide variety of mechanisms ranging from valve actuation in an automotive engine to sash locks in traditional double-hung windows. Cams are ordinarily used to convert rotary motion to linear (or almost linear) motion. As the cam rotates, the follower is forced to rise up and down, “following” the surface of the cam. Cams are most often used where the required motion would be difficult - or impossible - to achieve with a simple linkage. It is much more expensive and time-consuming to manufacture a precision cam than it is to fabricate ordinary links, so their use is mostly restricted to situations where linkages cannot achieve the required motion.
